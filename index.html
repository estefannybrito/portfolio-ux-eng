CASE 01 · B2B · SMALL-SCALE RETAIL

Transforming a B2B platform into the go-to tool for small businesses.

Product decisions were being driven by usage metrics and internal hypotheses without understanding merchants. The study revealed real motivations and four behavioral archetypes that became backlog inputs and prioritization criteria.

UX Researcher / PMRole
10 weeksDuration
230 + 10Participants + ethnographies
DiscoveryJTBD · Diary · Ethnography
SituationActive user base, but challenges around continued adoption and feature prioritization.
QuestionsMotivations, operational pain points, platform indispensability, and roadmap opportunities.
My roleOperational execution, research, JTBD, and stakeholder management.

Research strategy: a multi-method approach

01Diary study
230 participants · 3 weeks
02Human dimension
dreams, fears, goals
03Customer dimension
management and pain points
04Ethnography
10 merchants
05JTBD + synthesis
prioritization

The questions behind the research

What really motivates these entrepreneurs?Which operational pain points affect their day-to-day?How could the platform become indispensable?Which opportunities should be prioritized in the roadmap?

Results

INSIGHT 01

The goal was stability, not growth.

The main motivation was not rapid growth, but financial stability and quality of life for the family.

INSIGHT 02

Digitalization is a means, not an end.

Technology was seen as a way to save time, reduce operational effort, and create peace of mind.

INSIGHT 03

Four behavioral archetypes.

Community-Rooted, Resilient Builder, Strategic Optimizer, and Tired Achiever.

Community-Rooted

Local relationships and trust.

Resilient Builder

Survival and stability.

Strategic Optimizer

Efficiency and profit.

Tired Achiever

Financial success, but operational overload.

PRODUCT IMPACT

Research → backlog → prioritization criteria.

The study generated retention and engagement directions and opportunities such as inventory management automation, learning paths for first-time merchants, and improvements to communication and support.

Learning

Small business owners do not adopt technology simply because they like technology: they adopt it when it reduces real operational friction and frees time for what they value. As a limitation, the study focused on Brazil's Southeast region.

CASE 02 · GLOBAL MESSAGING PLATFORM

Redesigning the first interaction between consumers and businesses.

Inconsistent entry experiences created uncertainty and abandonment before the first message. Moderated tests with 30 participants across 3 countries showed that trust comes before convenience.

UX ResearcherRole
4 semanasDuration
30Participants
3 countriesBrazil · India · Germany
GoalReduce the risk of a global design change.
MethodsModerated usability testing + in-depth interviews.
AnalysisTrust · Control · Privacy.

The three pillars of the experience

TRUSTWhat makes a company feel legitimate?
CONTROLDoes the user understand what happens next?
PRIVACYConcerns about data exposure.

Insights

01

Trust comes before convenience.

The main factor behind sending the first message was trust, not speed.

02

The automation paradox.

Automation created a positive first impression, but too much of it raised concerns that no human support would be available.

03

Privacy is an abandonment trigger.

Concern about sharing personal data with unfamiliar companies was an important conversion barrier.

04

Legitimacy signals reduce friction.

A validated trust component was the element most often used as evidence that the company was legitimate.

PRODUCT IMPACT

Evidence to guide the redesign before implementation.

Global principles for the entry experience, balancing automation, privacy, and trust, while reducing risk in a large-scale change.

Learning

Users do not start conversations simply because they can: they do so when they understand who is on the other side and feel in control. The project deepened my experience in international research, concept comparison, mental models, and multicultural insight synthesis.

CASE 03 · USABILITY BENCHMARK

Usability barriers in ad management platforms.

A moderated benchmark across 6 ad platforms, with 90-minute sessions, think-aloud, and a structured codebook.

Research OpsRole
4 semanasFieldwork
6Plataformas
3 countriesBrasil · EUA · Índia
Core questionWhich platform requires the least cognitive effort for critical activities?
MethodsModerated usability · interviews · think-aloud · UserZoom.
ActivitiesCampaigns, budgeting, targeting, attribution, messaging, and payments.

How the benchmark was conducted

TASKSReal management activities.
OBSERVEBehavior and think-aloud.
CODEStructured codebook.
COMPAREPatterns across platforms.
DEBRIEFValidation with stakeholders.

Insights

01

Complexity affects small businesses more.

Less experienced users felt more uncertain around budgeting and payments.

02

Jargon creates avoidable errors.

Many participants understood the task goal but struggled to interpret the terminology.

03

Lack of predictability reduces confidence.

In mobile flows, it was difficult to understand where changes would be applied.

04

Perceived efficiency matters as much as actual efficiency.

Even when completing tasks, participants described the process as tiring.

PRODUCT IMPACT

Simplify flows, reduce cognitive load, and improve predictability.

The benchmark provided evidence for naming, advanced functionality, and consistency across environments, along with a clear comparison against competitors.

Operational learning

Preparing test accounts was critical for safely exploring complex tools. Dependence on the session tool, which experienced instability, was a fieldwork challenge to manage.

CASE 04 · SAFETY + TRUST

Safety and trust in Brazil's digital advertising ecosystem.

A multi-method study with 39 in-depth interviews across 5 segments, from industry experts to scam victims.

Victim consumers

10 consumers.

Results

PADRÃO 01

Cultural triggers.

Social proof and scarcity are highly powerful in Brazil and are used by scammers.

PADRÃO 02

Small advertiser vulnerability.

People who manage their own accounts have more difficulty interpreting policy updates and may only discover changes after an account is blocked.

PADRÃO 03

Scam fatigue.

Advertisers and agencies report scam attempts almost weekly, creating a constant state of alert that affects productivity.

RECOMMENDATIONS

Communicate policies beyond the dashboard, prioritize the Southeast, and strengthen reporting.

The central learning was that advertising safety is not only a technical issue: it is also cultural and requires mechanisms to adapt to local behaviors.

E-COMMERCE · BRAZIL + MEXICO

Homepage redesign for a global e-commerce platform.

Role:UX researcher in Brazil, paired with a Mexican researcher and two project leads.

Method:24 in-person interviews, think-aloud, and competitive benchmarking.

Finding:Lack of clear pricing in product cards was the biggest engagement barrier.

Recommendations:original and discounted prices, local personalization, and standardized descriptions.

B2B HEALTH · BRAZIL

B2B platform for healthcare professionals.

Role:Project Manager, operational coordination, and communication with global stakeholders.

Method:15 interviews, think-aloud, card sorting, and mirrored tasks with competitors.

Findings:lack of search was a critical failure; complex menus were rejected; samples were the main access hook.

Recommendations:global search, new taxonomy, a more visual design, and centralized sample requests.
